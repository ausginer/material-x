# M-1 — the move path, and I-26's honest number

**Status: answered, 2026-08-02. F-24's claim survives, with a bound it did not
have.**

M-1 ([05](../contract/05-lifecycle-invariants.md) §Measurements owed) asks
whether the generic frame copy on the pointer path costs anything a specialized
publication would save, and what one pointer sample actually costs.

## Harness

[`packages/drag2/tests/perf/m1.browser.test.ts`](../../../packages/drag2/tests/perf/m1.browser.test.ts),
checked in. Structural assertions — including the equivalence check — run in CI
on every suite run; the timings are opt-in with `VITE_DRAG_MEASURE=1` and assert
nothing.

- **Engine.** Chromium (Chrome for Testing 150) via
  `@vitest/browser-playwright`, headless, on the devcontainer CPU. Absolute
  numbers are machine-specific; every conclusion rests on a ratio or on an
  order of magnitude.
- **Policy.** 5 discarded warm-up iterations; a calibrated batch (doubling until
  a sample clears 2ms, because `performance.now()` is clamped to 100µs); 21
  samples; **median**, since a mean is dominated by its first iteration and by
  GC.
- **Variants.** `generic` is what ships — `beginFrame` (an `Object.assign` over
  the whole frame), two field writes, swap. `specialized` writes the two pointer
  fields into the committed frame: no copy, no swap.
- **Equivalence.** 64 samples through both variants must leave byte-identical
  committed frames, asserted before any timing is quoted. A second assertion
  pins the *bound*: the specialization is only valid for a sample that changes
  nothing else, so it could be a second path, never the only one.
- **Frame shapes.** Behavior part sizes 3, 8, 12, 16, 20 and 28, plus a
  polymorphic scenario that drives three shapes through one call site — the
  concern F-24 actually raises.

**Run one measurement file at a time.** The browser project runs test files in
parallel, and two measurement suites sharing a page inflate every absolute
figure by roughly 2× (the shipped-shape copy reads 0.214 µs instead of
0.098 µs). Ratios and the cliff location survive it; absolutes do not. Every
number quoted here comes from a run of this file alone:

```
VITE_DRAG_MEASURE=1 npx vitest run -c vitest.config.ts \
  --project browser tests/perf/m1.browser.test.ts
```

The shipped frame is **8 part fields** (`SortableFramePart`) on top of 7 kernel
fields: the "15-field `Object.assign`" the plan names.

## Numbers

Per publication, median:

| part fields | generic | specialized | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 0.061 µs | 0.004 µs | 15× |
| **8 — what ships** | **0.098 µs** | **0.005 µs** | **20×** |
| 12 | 0.147 µs | 0.005 µs | 27× |
| 16 | 1.465 µs | 0.011 µs | 128× |
| 20 | 1.758 µs | 0.011 µs | 154× |
| 28 | 2.148 µs | 0.011 µs | 188× |
| polymorphic (3/8/20 through one site) | 0.586 µs | 0.015 µs | 40× |

End to end, one dispatched `pointermove` through a live drag:

| rows | per sample |
| --- | --- |
| 50 | 2.64 µs |
| 200 | 2.73 µs |

Allocation: **20,000 consecutive samples produced no measurable net heap
growth** (`--enable-precise-memory-info`, no intervening `gc()`).

## Answer 1 — the copy stays, and now there is a number

At the shipped shape the copy is **0.098 µs of a 2.64 µs sample: 3.7%**. The
sample itself is **0.017% of a 16 ms frame**. Removing the copy would buy
~0.09 µs per sample while splitting frame publication into two paths — one
transactional, one not — and the second path is only legal for samples that
change nothing but the pointer, which the kernel would have to prove at every
call site.

So F-24's *"removing the copy would be performance theatre"* is **correct**, and
it now says so with a number instead of a conviction.

## Answer 2 — but the claim is bounded, and the bound was not known

The copy's cost is **not linear in frame size**. It is flat and cheap to ~12 part
fields and then jumps **10×** between 12 and 16 — 0.147 µs to 1.465 µs — after
which it grows slowly again. That is an engine cliff (V8's fast path for
`Object.assign` over objects with few enough in-object properties), not a
property of the contract, but the contract lives on the cheap side of it by 4
fields.

**This is the part F-24 could not have known by inspection.** A behavior with a
larger frame part — or a second behavior sharing the kernel — crosses it, and the
copy goes from 3.7% of a sample to roughly 55%. The claim is therefore recorded
as *the copy is free at this frame size*, with the size named.

The polymorphic case costs 0.586 µs — 6× the monomorphic 8-field figure, and
between the two sides of the cliff, as expected when one call site sees three
shapes. It is still 22% of a sample. Several controllers of *different behaviors*
on one page pay this; several controllers of the *same* behavior do not, because
the shape is the same.

## Answer 3 — I-26

I-26 claims one pointer sample "creates no wrapper, tuple, collection or
protocol object". 20,000 samples produced no measurable net heap growth, which
**bounds retention at zero and transient allocation below the noise floor of a
precise-memory reading**.

It is not the same statement as the invariant. "Creates no object" is about
allocation, and a collector that keeps up makes allocation invisible to a heap
reading. The stronger form stays what 06 says it is — a claim established by
inspection — and this is the executable evidence that nothing contradicts it.

## What would reopen this

- A behavior part above ~12 fields, or a second behavior whose part pushes the
  shared call site over the cliff.
- A frame that stops being flat scalars: the copy is shallow, so its cost is the
  field count, not the data.
- An engine whose `Object.assign` has a different cliff, or none. The 12-to-16
  boundary is Chromium's and is not a portable constant.
