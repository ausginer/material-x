## The D-174 specimen deviation, tested against the rule rather than the specimen

D-174's phase-24 record illustrates the satellite rewrite as `### F-2 — part factory determinism · open, tier C` → `### Part factory determinism (F-2)` — dropping the status suffix. The landed form instead keeps the suffix on all 48 headings: `### Part factory determinism · open, tier C (F-2)` (verified at 05:317 and by a full count of 48 `### … (F-n)`-shaped headings across 05/07, plus `02`'s `I-31`).

Tested against D-174's actual preservation rule — "the whole analysis, verbatim… nothing is deleted" (d174 record §4) — rather than against the specimen text: dropping the suffix uniformly is the thing that would have violated the rule. A word-by-word check (recorded in `plan.md`, the entry at `00-index.md:1561`) found 46 of 48 suffixes are pure restatement of the canonical entry's own status and 2 are not — F-1's _not a finding_ and F-16's _scenario superseded by D-41, classification inherited by D-49_ — text that appears nowhere else in the record. Retaining all 48 uniformly, rather than selectively dropping the 46 "safe" ones, satisfies the verbatim-preservation invariant without requiring a per-entry judgment call that the disposition itself would then have to defend line by line; it costs only a duplicated word on the 46. The specimen was illustrative of the _structural_ move (identifier out of key position) and was never itself a preservation requirement — its incidental deletion of the suffix is the one part of it this pass correctly declined to generalize. No property from the numbered list is weakened by this choice, and the two sites (specimen text vs. landed text) disagree on wording only, not on any content the record requires to survive.
