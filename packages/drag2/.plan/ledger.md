# Parity ledger — `@ydinjs/drag` → `@ydinjs/drag2`

Phase 12 deliverable. One row per public capability of the shipped package's two entries, classified **retain / redesign / drop**, with a destination.

This is the definition of "parity" every later phase cites. Where a phase asks "does the successor have to do this?", the answer is a row here, not a judgement made in flight.

**Precedent.** [`../../drag/.plan/phase-1/08-compatibility-ledger.md`](../../drag/.plan/phase-1/08-compatibility-ledger.md) is the same instrument, written for the shipped package's own redesign. It is the form this follows.

## How to read a row

| Class | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **retain** | The capability exists in the successor with the same consumer-visible shape. |
| **redesign** | The capability exists; the shape a consumer writes changes. The row says what changes. |
| **drop** | The capability is not offered. The row says **what a consumer loses**. |

A third column, **shape**, may read _deferred to Phase N_. That is a complete row: the capability is decided here, its public decomposition is not, because deciding it needs evidence a later phase produces. What is never permitted is a capability with no class.

**Verified against** `packages/drag/src/{draggable,sortable}.ts`, their `options.ts`, `kernel/{types,protocol}.ts`, both runtime controllers, and the `tests/` corpus, at commit `5c1a14a1`.

**Amended for Revision 2 — the API redesign, D-36…D-60, 2026-08-13, and reconciled at Revision 2.1 — D-61…D-67.** Five parity rows move at 2.1: `onFinish`/`onCancel` become one `onEnd` (D-62), `createPlaceholder` becomes `placeholder` (D-65), `onError`'s context loses its stage for a coarse code (D-64), and `landing({ run })` is withdrawn (D-63) — which puts **L-6 under pressure**, because the thunk is then its sole carrier. [`contract/00-index.md`](contract/00-index.md) §Revision 2 is authoritative; this ledger records what each decision does to **parity**, which is a different question and occasionally a less comfortable one. Four rows change class, two constructor rows are restated under D-48, six counted rows and one uncounted addition are new, one section is new (§5.1), three findings are retracted or rescoped in place, and §10 is reconciled. **One consequence was left open, and is now closed** — the ledger flagged that D-48's original wording re-sited `drag.js` as the kernel entrypoint while that entry carries the vocabulary an ordinary `onError` handler depends on; D-48 is corrected before implementation, `drag.js` keeps its job and a new `kernel.js` carries the authoring tier, and the three affected rows in §7 are _retain_. The near-miss is recorded there because it is the kind a parity question catches and a tier argument does not. **A parity ledger that only records what a successor keeps is doing half its job** — Revision 2 is the first revision to take working behavior away from a shipped consumer, and §L-13 and §5.1 exist so that is stated rather than discovered. Retractions keep their original reasoning visible; where a row was reversed, the reversal and the evidence that forced it are recorded rather than the row quietly rewritten (§L-1 is the worked example).

**Closed against shipped code, 2026-08-14.** Every row below now describes `packages/drag2/src` rather than a decision about it: D-36…D-67 are implemented and gated. The reconciliation pass changed **two** rows and added **one**, all of them in the terminal's neighbourhood, because D-62 and D-66 landed after this ledger was last amended — `SortableFinishResult`/`SortableCancelResult` move _retain_ → **drop** (§2.1), the rejection row is restated over `onEnd`'s arm (§3), and a **new §3 row** records that drag2 now publishes a terminal where the shipped package retires in silence. The coverage recount at the end carries the arithmetic. Nothing else moved, and that is the useful part: the rows amended for Revision 2 were written against the contract, and the contract is what was implemented.

## 5. Sortable — two-dimensional insertion

Classified **retain**. Public decomposition **decided and shipped at Phase 17** — the deferral is closed (D7, Checkpoint D).

**The shape: a sibling axis feature, `xy()`, on `sortable/xy.js`.** Of the three candidates named below, the one with shipped precedent — an unrestricted 2-D default that an axis feature narrows — cannot be built without breaking consequence 3: a default lives in the behavior core and cannot be tree-shaken, so every list consumer would carry the 2-D metric _and_ the narrowing feature. One parameterized axis feature fails the same rule ~120 B more cheaply and in the same direction. Two subpaths keep each composition paying for its own rule.

The axis features are also **renamed to the axes they measure**: `vertical()` → `y()` on `sortable/y.js`, with `x()` reserved. That is a breaking public change, the second in Part II after D-33's `ResolutionOptions`, and it is recorded in 03 §The export topology this requires. The paragraphs below keep `vertical()` where they are describing what was known when the row was written; `sortable/vertical.ts` **no longer exists** and the module referred to is `sortable/y.ts`.

Measured cost: `minimal` 9.90 → 9.96 kB for the shared rect index; `minimal (xy)` measured as a peer at 10.01 kB rather than assumed equal. The 1-D case does not pay for the 2-D one, which is the constraint the decision was made under.

The material finding that led there, unchanged: **the shipped package has no grid capability, because it has no axis concept at all**:

- `nearestSlot` (`sortable/rect-index.ts:145`) is a squared-Euclidean search over both centre coordinates. `RectIndex` packs `centreX` _and_ `centreY`.
- `SortableOptions` has **no `axis` member**. The Grid story is the List story with different CSS; its hint — "a wrapping grid is one field of rectangles, so any direction works" — is literally the implementation.
- Gap derivation is DOM-order (`follows`), not geometric, so it is already dimension-independent.

So the successor's relationship to the shipped package is inverted from how the roadmap has been describing it: drag2's `vertical()` is a **narrowing** of shipped behavior, and 2-D is the shipped default. Consequences:

1. This is a **retain** row, not a new feature. "Grid support" is the removal of a restriction drag2 introduced, not the addition of one the shipped package had.
2. The 1-D rule consumes exactly one frame field, `pointerY` (now `sortable/y.ts`). A 2-D rule needs `pointerX` as well — the second consumer-declared-view widening, and the direct test of whether D-13's view mechanism generalises or whether phase 8a's widening was a one-off. **Answered at Phase 17: it generalised.** Both widenings were additive and satisfied structurally by the behavior's existing frame, with no wrapper, no allocation and no import edge back to the runtime. Two data points, so the honest reading is a _growing_ structural contract rather than a fixed one. Feeds Checkpoint E.
3. Whether the public shape is a `grid()` sibling, a parameterization, or an unrestricted default that the 1-D rule constrains. **Decided above: the first**, against the shipped precedent, because only it satisfies "the 1-D case must not pay for the 2-D one". `y()` is also not `xy()` with an axis switched off — in a single column, a horizontal excursion adds the same X term to every candidate and the squared sum lets it swamp the Y ordering near a boundary, so the suite pins a sideways drag that `xy()` reorders and `y()` proposes nothing for.

### 5.1 Layout scope limits — D-43, Revision 2

D-43 states three limits positively. Each is checked against the shipped package here, because a limit a predecessor honoured is a **drop** and a limit it merely never expressed is a **statement of scope**, and the two are not the same row.

| Limit | Shipped | Class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The container's **visual order must follow DOM order** | **Never held there either, and the incoherence is in one function.** `resolveSpatialInsertion` (`packages/drag/src/sortable/insertion.ts:25-61`) picks the nearest candidate **geometrically** — `nearestSlot` is a squared-Euclidean search over both centres — and then derives the gap **from DOM order**, `follows(placeholder, nearest) ? slot + 1 : slot`, where `follows` is a `compareDocumentPosition` test. Where visual order and DOM order agree the two agree; where they diverge the shipped package emits a proposal built half from geometry and half from document position, with no complaint. | **not a drop — an undefined case now stated as out of contract.** What a consumer loses is the appearance of support. |
| **Rule-placed layouts** — where a DOM insertion changes unrelated visual placement — are unsupported | Same function, same reason. Reordering the DOM under `grid-auto-flow: dense`, explicit `grid-row`/`grid-column`, or CSS `order` reflows items the proposal never named, so the committed result is not the previewed one. Nothing in `sortable/` reads a computed placement. | **not a drop.** |
| In CSS Grid, **`box === visual` is required** — a nested lifted visual that leaves a separate `box` occupying the grid cell is unsupported | Unreachable in shipped: there is no `box` concept, and the placeholder is sized from `visual.offsetWidth`/`offsetHeight` (`runtime/actions.ts:550-551`). The nested-visual grid case is exactly where that sizing is wrong (F-50), so shipped does not support it — it renders it wrongly. | **not a drop.** The limit is a scope statement attached to a capability D-43 **adds**. |

**All three are statements, not losses**, and that is the finding: every layout D-43 declares unsupported is one the shipped package also failed at, silently, by producing a plausible-looking proposal from two incompatible orderings. **What Revision 2 changes is the failure mode, not the coverage** — from a wrong result with no signal to a documented boundary. D-43 adds no detection and no repair, deliberately (§Declined scope in [`plan.md`](plan.md)), so a consumer in one of these layouts still gets the wrong result; it now gets it against a written contract that says so.

## 9. Findings

Numbered so later phases can cite them.

- **L-1 — the `items()` thunk was never re-read, and the conclusion drawn from that is reversed by D-44.** The **observation stands and was verified**: called once, at construction; `updateItems` is the only other path; a reference search on the member found 53 references, of which exactly one is an invocation (`sortable/runtime/controller.ts:109`) — every other is an option-object declaration in the tests or the stories. ~~The array-argument redesign is behavior-preserving, not a simplification with a cost.~~

  **What was wrong was the inference.** From "the thunk is never re-read" this finding concluded "the parameter is dead, so remove it", and D-44 concludes the opposite from the same fact: **the thunk was never re-read because the package carried two collection channels and re-read neither.** One source that is pulled, plus one payload-free signal that says when to pull, collapses them — and it collapses them onto the channel this finding proposed to delete. Deleting the thunk would have left `updateItems` as the sole channel and pushed every consumer into a push discipline; deleting `updateItems` leaves the thunk as the sole channel and lets the consumer keep its own state as the single source of truth.

  **The reason the reversal is right is a fact about consumers that this finding never looked for.** Array identity is a **free structural test** — React, Vue and Svelte all hand back a new array when order changes, so the consumer's framework is already producing the exact signal the library needs to distinguish "geometry moved" from "membership changed". A push API cannot see that signal without being told; a pull API gets it by asking. That also moves the O(n) shallow copy off every warm invalidation — a resize, a zoom — and onto structural change only, which is where the earlier reading had no opinion at all because it had no second call to have one about.

  **This is the clearest case in the corpus of a decision reversed by later evidence, and it is recorded rather than rewritten for that reason.** The finding was not sloppy: it counted call sites, it verified them, and it drew the narrowest conclusion the count supported. What it could not see from a call-site count is _why_ the count was one, and the answer was a design defect in the thing being counted rather than a property of the parameter. **A reference search tells you what is used. It does not tell you what should have been.**

- **L-12 — the terminal barrier is behavior-owned, and this is not a parity row. Its _proof domain_ is retired by D-37 (Revision 2); its floor and its parity classification are not.** Added at Checkpoint D review 2 (C2-01).

  **Read the Revision 2 note first, because the rest of this finding is written against a domain that no longer exists.** D-37 replaces the whole-program quantifier with a finite, API-shaped one: after logical closure the library must not (a) invoke a **declared consumer slot**, (b) **admit** another operation, or (c) **publish** another lifecycle or domain event. The consumer reach/stretch analysis — provisioning, the five-act floor, the register of named ceilings, and the 62-row stretch table it terminated in — is retired **as a proof domain**. What survives is the defect this finding actually found and the fix that closed it: a sequence that calls consumer code repeatedly reads the latch between calls. Every landed reading stays; nothing is removed from `src/`.

  **Clause (a) has exactly one stated exception, and stating it is what keeps the domain finite (D-51).** A call into a declared consumer slot after logical closure is permitted **only** when its sole purpose is to release a resource the library holds and cannot release itself; it performs no operation work, publishes nothing, ignores the return value, and is wrapped. **The list is currently one member, `LandingHandle.destroy()`**, and adding to it is a contract change. Without the exception D-37 is false in the one place it must not be — the kernel _must_ destroy a landing handle returned by a runner that destroyed the controller, or it leaks a runner nothing owns (I-20, F-30) — and an unstated exception is worse here than the prose floor that openly admitted the breach, because **a finite domain with an unstated exception is not finite**, and being checkable is the whole value of D-37. The discriminating property is that relinquishment _returns_ something to the consumer rather than _asking_ anything of it, which is what stops the exception being used to smuggle operation work past a close. Under D-36 a relinquishing call is part of physical teardown and defers with it.

  **L-12 was right about the problem, and that is why it is worth reading after the retirement rather than instead of it.** Three paragraphs down, in its own words, this finding says the real set is every DOM method the library calls on a consumer-owned node "**which does not terminate as a list**" — it diagnosed the undischargeability of its own obligation, correctly, and then tried to discharge it anyway, first by narrowing the rule three times, then by making it structural ("a reading at the head of every loop"), then by sweeping 62 stretches to prove the structural rule had been applied. **D-37's answer is to change the domain, not to keep enumerating.** A bracket makes an act consequence-free by _undoing_ it; for a call the consequence **is** the call, so exactly the declared-slot invocations are irreducible residue and everything else the old quantifier ranged over was already covered by the transaction boundary. The finite list is one the export table already enumerates, so "has this been discharged" becomes a question with a mechanical answer instead of a sweep that is only ever as complete as its last reviewer.

  **What this costs, stated honestly:** the published ceiling goes. drag2 no longer claims that no geometry is read after a close, only that no declared slot is invoked, no operation is admitted and nothing is published. The narrower claim is checkable and the wider one was not, and an unverifiable guarantee in a README is worth less than a verifiable one — but it is a smaller promise, and F-47 records it as a withdrawal rather than as a clarification.

  **The parity classification is untouched.** This was never a parity row and is not one now: the shipped `rebuildRectIndex` has no liveness check either, so every reading here is an improvement on shipped rather than a repair of a drag2 regression, and D-37 changes only how much of that improvement is claimed. Original finding follows. A consumer resolver invoked in a sequence — `getHandle` then `getVisual` at admission, `getVisual` once per candidate inside the axis cache, a placeholder custom element's reaction inside a committed `movePlaceholder` — could call `controller.destroy()` and the sequence carried on, calling more consumer code after teardown returned and, in the candidate loop, resurrecting a cache `retire()` had just emptied.   **Three passes narrowing the same barrier is itself the finding — and after D-37, the sentence in the middle of it is the finding.** The enumeration had been derived by listing consumer _callbacks_, and under I-36's indirect-invocation clause the real set is every DOM method the library calls on a consumer-owned node — **which does not terminate as a list.** That observation is stated here, in this finding, at the moment the register was being built, and it is the exact reason D-37 later withdrew the domain rather than adding a fourth narrowing: an obligation whose subject does not terminate as a list cannot be discharged by any implementation or checked by any review, however structural the rule that ranges over it. The three narrowings and the sweep below are what it costs to keep trying. C4-01 therefore made the rule structural instead: a reading at the **head of every loop that touches consumer-owned nodes**, after each consumer-reachable call inside it, and before the next foreign call, DOM mutation or state publication; and every participant that touches consumer DOM is handed a liveness reading rather than having one site patched. `DisplacementView` gained `live` — the **fifth** additive widening of a consumer-declared view, which C2-01 §9.5 had already recorded as a routine act.   **What it obliges later phases — rewritten by D-37.** The floor is not sortable-specific and still holds: any behavior that calls consumer code more than once inside one seam or one native admission reads the latch between calls. Phase 18 therefore still carries a deliverable to enumerate **every declared consumer slot free drag's behavior invokes** and state where its terminal barrier is — a list its own option surface produces, ending at the same three categories D-37 names. What Phase 18 no longer inherits is the stretch decomposition, the provisioning/floor/register apparatus and the "does anything the stretch does survive the stretch" sweep; those were built to discharge a quantifier that no longer exists, and copying them forward would import the undischargeable obligation into the second behavior. The stated falsifier for keeping the latch behavior-owned rather than kernel-supplied is unchanged: a **third** copy of it.

- **L-13 — the input policy is the only place drag2 refuses input the shipped package accepted, and it is release-blocking in the other direction.** Added at Revision 2, from probe E and D-46. The three §4 rows and the §3 `preventDefault()` row are, jointly, the largest deliberate parity narrowing in this ledger: an arrow inside a text input no longer reorders, an IME composition arrow never admits, and a press on an interactive or editable descendant is declined rather than consumed. **Each is the removal of a defect and each is still a removal**, which is the distinction this ledger exists to keep. The shipped evidence is unambiguous — probe E found **eight of ten cases release-blocking, six with no drag ever activating at all** — so the alternative to narrowing is shipping a package whose rows cannot contain a working form control.

  **The asymmetry is worth naming.** On the pointer side drag2 gives up almost nothing real: only `pointerdown` was ever prevented, so click, `href` navigation and ctrl-click were never at risk, and the decline path is already total. On the keyboard side it gives up an actual working path — the shipped `admitCommand` asks four questions and none of them is "what did this land on" (`packages/drag/src/sortable/runtime/controller.ts:192-220`) — so a shipped integration where rows contain inputs was reorderable from inside them and will not be. **That is the row a migration guide has to lead with**, and the answer is a focusable grip, which §4 now records as an obligation rather than leaving to be discovered.

- **L-14 — the kernel tier has drag2's own instance of L-2, and documenting it is what found it.** Added at Revision 2.1 closure. `kernel.js` has shipped since D-48 and was **absent from `typedoc.json`**, so the one tier whose whole purpose is to be written against was the one tier no unresolved-reference warning could reach. Adding it produced **17**: `BehaviorSpec`, `KernelHost` and `BehaviorFactory` structurally name `Frame`, `Draft`, `Transition`, `LiftMode`, the five `SETTLED_*` codes and eight more, none of them exported.

  **This is not a parity row and cannot become one** — the shipped package has no custom-behavior tier at all (§1), so there is nothing to be at parity with — which is precisely why it needs a finding: a gap against nothing shipped is invisible to every other instrument in this document. It is the same defect L-2 catalogues in the predecessor, freshly minted in the successor, at the tier the successor added.

  **What it costs a behavior author today is bounded but real.** A factory written inline is contextually typed and names none of the 17; a spec object hoisted into its own `const`, or a helper that takes a `Frame`, cannot be annotated. So the tier is writable in one style and not in another, which is the same shape as L-2's "a consumer cannot annotate its own `onError`".

  **Deliberately not fixed at closure.** Exporting them is an addition to a frozen surface and it pre-empts D-47 §11's queued _minimize the kernel vocabulary_ work, which decides what an author should have to name — a design question, not a documentation one. Closure states the gap instead: the 17 are declared file-qualified in `typedoc.json` under `intentionallyNotExported`, TypeDoc fails the run when one of them stops being referenced or becomes exported, and the list therefore shrinks itself as that pass lands. **The lesson is the one about instruments, not the one about types:** an entry point missing from the docs config is a surface that cannot be checked, and no amount of strictness elsewhere compensates.

**Reconciled against Revision 2, 2026-08-13; re-checked at closure, 2026-08-14.** Neither question below is answered by D-36…D-67, and both still belong to Phase 18. What Revision 2 does change is the frame the second one is decided in, and it adds two questions of its own.

Phase 18 still decides.

**Closed by Revision 2, and recorded so they are not re-asked:**

- **What happens when a consumer rerenders destructively mid-drag.** Answered by D-42: out of contract, precondition checked in O(1), reported through `onError`, nothing recovered. §3 carries the row.
- **Whether the readiness deadline is a public option.** Answered by deletion (D-41). §7 carries the drop, including the capability genuinely lost — a _bounded_ wait.

**Added at Revision 2 — the two falsifiers in [`contract/00-index.md`](contract/00-index.md) §What would falsify this model that are the owner's rather than the contract's.** The other two added there (the variadic merge's required-slot completeness as a type error; a behavior needing to observe its own teardown from inside the deferral window) are questions about what the type system and the SPI can express, and belong to the contract and the implementation, not here.

3. **Is "moves existing item nodes" the right line for the frameworks you intend to support?** D-42 draws the supported/destructive boundary there, and probe C1 shows `ReorderRequest.before`/`after` survive connected and mutually sibling in **four of five** strategies, so recovery is _buildable_ and is being declined rather than being impossible. If a mainstream framework's committed output cannot be expressed as node movement — a renderer that legitimately replaces the container, or one whose keyed path still recreates the dragged node — the line is in the wrong place, and reopening it also reopens D-27's cross-container refusal for the post-commit path. **This is a product-scope question, not an architectural one**, which is why it is here: the contract can hold either line, and only the owner knows which integrations must work.
