# M-5 — the unconditional activation work neither behavior needs twice

**Status: run 2026-08-20.** **Arm A: F-65 closes as an accepted, named cost.** At the deep transformed ancestry, D-52's window 1 is **not resolvable in situ** and is bounded at **≤ 0.0125 ms**, with a direct measurement of its two reads at **0.8–1.6 µs**; the share is **≤ 2.7%** of measured activation. Both legs of the conjunction are satisfied, so **the `ActivationScope` SPI does not move again for this reason**. **Arm B: D-85's recorded ground is confirmed.** The `inheritedSpace` derivation costs **≤ 6 ns** per activation — about **0.001%** of the sortable's activation — and performs **zero** layout-facing reads. **No SPI question opens. No Phase 22 implementation work.**

M-5 ([`phase-21.md`](phase-21.md) §M-5) is the only genuinely new measurement in Phase 21. F-65 had been carried since Phase 18 with _Phase 21 measures it; Checkpoint E decides_, and Checkpoint E closed without the number.

## The two arms

**Arm A — D-52's window 1 (F-65).** `boxPre` is the box's offset box, read by the kernel immediately before `acquireLift` so a behavior can derive a placeholder footprint from two reads straddling acquisition. **Free drag has no footprint, takes no second read and never names `boxPre`** — the read happens anyway, because it is unconditional kernel work.

**Arm B — D-85's `inheritedSpace`.** The inverse inherited linear part, derived from the measurement `acquireLift` already took, published on every `ActivationScope`. **The sortable never names it.** D-85 was accepted on the ground that this is _arithmetic over a materialized buffer rather than a layout read_; **this arm checks that ground, and cannot reopen the decision.** D-85 is a correctness fix — one traversal, two products, because a second traversal reads an ancestry acquisition has already mutated — and no timing result removes `inheritedSpace`.

## The decision rule, applied without reinterpretation

The rule was fixed before the run in [`phase-21.md`](phase-21.md) §The decision rule, and is quoted rather than re-derived:

| Outcome | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| **F-65 closes** | **under 0.2 ms** absolute **and** **under 5%** of measured activation |
| **The SPI question opens** | **over 0.5 ms** absolute, whatever the share |
| **Indeterminate** | anything else, including a small absolute with a large share |

**The conjunction is asymmetric and stays so.** A share can **block** a close; it can never **open** the SPI. Both thresholds are fractions of a display-fixed 16 ms frame — 1.2% and 3% — because activation is once per drag and the only budget a once-per-drag cost has is the frame the drag starts in.

**The quantity is read at the deep transformed ancestry.** The shallow figures below are context and are not read by the rule; a box-quad traversal is depth-sensitive, and a shallow tree can make either arm unmeasurable and prove nothing about a real page.

### The denominator, as D-96 defines it

_Measured activation_ is **the threshold-crossing `pointermove` dispatch, end to end**: the kernel acquires the origin rect, reads window 1, acquires the lift, takes pointer capture and runs the activation seam, and the behavior's `activation.effect` runs inside it.

**No consumer slot is inside it, by construction of the fixture.** Free drag composes `onDrop` alone — which runs at release, not activation — and the sortable composes `items` (an O(1) return of a stable array), `axis: y()` (a library feature, not a consumer source) and `onReorder` (release). **No `onStart`, no `onMove`, no `visual` resolver, no `box` resolver.** With consumer work in the denominator a fixture author moves the share to either side of any gate by writing more or less of it, which is a property of the fixture and not of the tree.

### The one deviation from q7's sampling policy, and why

q7's 2 ms batch target exists to clear the clock's 0.1 ms grain, and it does. **The deciding quantity here is a _difference_ two to three orders of magnitude below either side of it**, so a batch sized only to clear the grain leaves the difference inside the batch's own scheduling noise: the first paired run of this file returned a p5 of **−0.71 ms** on a 0.45 ms activation, which is one descheduled sample and not a measurement. The paired arm therefore calibrates to **30 ms** — 64 activations per batch at the deep ancestry, 512 at the shallow one. The statistic is unchanged; only the denominator of the quantization grows, and the resulting per-activation resolution (0.0016 ms deep, 0.0002 ms shallow) is printed with every row.

### The paired arm is one fixture with the stub toggled between samples

Measuring one whole arm and then the other lets the machine drift between them. **The first run of this file did exactly that** and read window 1 as **−0.0125 ms** at the shallow ancestry and **+0.0500 ms** at the deep one in the same session — a sign flip of the same quantity, which is the drift and not the read.

The stub is an accessor swap on a single element, so it can be toggled without rebuilding anything. One fixture, one controller, and each of the 21 samples contributes **its own** paired difference, with the order alternating per sample so a systematic first-in-pair advantage cancels rather than accumulating into the median.

**Only the measured fixture is live** (the Phase 21 rule): every arm builds, measures and disposes in a `finally` before the next is built.

### The stub removes the measured work and nothing else

**Arm A's control replaces the box element's `offsetWidth`/`offsetHeight` accessors.** `boxPre` is still built, still allocated and still published on the scope; what the stub removes is the pair of layout-facing reads that build it. **Both arms go through the same JS getter** — the shipped mode forwards to the native accessor, the stubbed mode returns a constant — so the difference is the native read and not the dispatch.

The lift's own width and height come from box-quad's traversal (`measured[BOX_WIDTH]`, `measured[BOX_HEIGHT]`), not from these accessors, so **acquisition is untouched**. That is what makes the equivalence check assertable rather than aspirational, and it is why the control does not change the semantic shape of activation.

**Equivalence, asserted before any ratio is quoted** — two rows, in CI:

- **Free drag's activation output is identical** with and without the stub, compared as the visual's whole inline style after activation, with a positive control (`position: fixed` must be present, so two empty strings cannot pass).
- **The sortable's activation output is _not_ identical**, compared as the visual's style **and the placeholder's** — because window 1's product lands on the placeholder, whose footprint is `boxPre` minus the collapsed box. The sortable **reads** window 1, so the stub is not a specialization there; it is a different behavior. **This is why arm A's number is free drag's**, which is the behavior the finding is about, and it is asserted rather than asserted-by-omission.

### Falsifying the instrument

A paired difference that is always near zero is indistinguishable from an instrument that cannot see anything. The falsifier puts a **known** cost in exactly the place window 1 occupies — forced reads injected **inside the offset-box accessors the kernel reads**, on the same fixture, through the same paired design — and requires the difference to move with it:

| injected reads | measured difference                       |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| 16             | **> 0.02 ms**, asserted                   |
| 128            | **> 3×** the 16-read difference, asserted |

Both rows assert on every suite run, not behind the measurement flag. The instrument is therefore known to resolve a cost at the scale of ~0.02 ms located exactly at window 1 — and window 1 itself measures below that floor.

## Result — arm A, D-52's window 1 (F-65)

**In situ, paired, five runs.** Median of 21 paired differences per run:

| ancestry | measured activation | window 1, per run | envelope | share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **deep transformed (decision-driving)** | 0.453 – 0.491 ms | +0.0031, −0.0109, +0.0125, 0.0000, +0.0031 ms | **\|·\| ≤ 0.0125 ms** | **\|·\| ≤ 2.7%** |
| shallow (telemetry) | 0.073 – 0.087 ms | −0.0014, +0.0033, +0.0031, −0.0070, +0.0023 ms | \|·\| ≤ 0.0070 ms | \|·\| ≤ 9.3% |

**The point estimate straddles zero at both depths: window 1 has no resolvable cost in situ.**

**Supporting, and it supplies the magnitude the null cannot.** The same sequence measured directly — a bounding rect, then the two offset reads, against a control that stops after the rect, with layout dirtied every iteration as a release dirties it:

| ancestry | rect + offsets | rect alone | **window 1** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| deep transformed | 8.6 – 9.0 µs | 7.4 – 8.2 µs | **0.78 – 1.56 µs** |
| shallow | 7.0 – 7.2 µs | 5.9 – 8.6 µs | −1.56 – 1.17 µs (at the arm's own ±1.5 µs floor) |

**0.8–1.6 µs is 0.0008–0.0016 ms**, which sits far inside the in-situ envelope and is exactly why the in-situ arm returns a null.

**The mechanism is in the source and the numbers agree with it.** `acquireActivation` reads `target.getBoundingClientRect()` on the statement **before** window 1, so the offset reads are served from a layout that has just been flushed. That is also why window 1 is nearly depth-insensitive while measured activation grows six-fold from shallow to deep: the traversal is depth-sensitive, and two reads of an already-clean layout are not.

### Why this is a bound and not a null quoted as one

The reproducibility standard's standing rule is that **a one-tick null is a null, not an upper bound**, and it is applied here rather than stepped around. What is quoted above is not a one-tick null:

- the paired median is taken over **21 differences per run at a per-activation resolution of 0.0016 ms**, which is the confidence interval on the difference the earlier rule said a real bound requires;
- **five runs** bracket that median inside ±0.0125 ms;
- an **independent** direct measurement returns a positive magnitude, 0.8–1.6 µs, that lies well inside the envelope and explains the null rather than merely being consistent with it;
- and the **falsifier** establishes that the same instrument resolves ~0.02 ms at the same location, so the null is a statement about the quantity and not about the instrument.

### The rule, applied

| Leg | Threshold | Measured | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| absolute | under **0.2 ms** | ≤ 0.0125 ms bound; 0.0008–0.0016 ms direct | **satisfied**, by two orders of magnitude |
| share | under **5%** | ≤ 2.7% bound; 0.2–0.3% direct | **satisfied** |

**F-65 closes as an accepted, named cost: window 1 is 0.8–1.6 µs per activation, bounded in situ at ≤ 0.0125 ms and ≤ 2.7% of measured activation at a 32-level transformed ancestry.** Per the rule, **the `ActivationScope` SPI does not move again for this reason, and a later phase may not reopen it without a new workload showing a different number.**

**The shallow share is 9.3% and decides nothing.** The rule reads the deep row, by its own terms; and a share computed on a difference that changes sign between runs is a share of the noise, not of the read. It is recorded because the contract asks for the shallow figures as context.

## Result — arm B, D-85's `inheritedSpace`

**The instrument, and why it is shaped this way.** `inheritedSpaceOf` is module-private and is called from inside `acquireLift`, so **an activation without it cannot be built without editing `src/`** — which the checked-in-harness rule does not allow for a shipped-path measurement. Rather than substitute a proxy, the arm follows M-1's discipline: a **line-for-line copy** measured against a control, held to an equivalence check first.

**The equivalence check is in situ and exact.** `LIFT_IN_PLACE` hands `inheritedSpace` straight to the session's `compose`, so `compose(1, 0)` and `compose(0, 1)` encode the four coefficients **the shipped private function produced for that element at that instant**. The copy must reproduce all four to nine decimal places on the deep transformed fixture, and the derivation must not have taken its early `null` branch — which is what makes the row able to fail. Asserted in CI.

**The control** reads the same four slots off the same buffer and returns their sum, so the buffer access is on both sides and the difference is the arithmetic alone — the identity comparison, the determinant, the four divides and the object.

|                      | five runs                                     |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| derivation           | 0.0092, 0.0099, 0.0092, 0.0095, 0.0153 µs     |
| control              | 0.0088, 0.0088, 0.0084, 0.0088, 0.0092 µs     |
| **`inheritedSpace`** | **0.0004, 0.0011, 0.0008, 0.0008, 0.0061 µs** |

**≤ 6 ns per activation**, against the sortable's measured activation of **0.425 – 0.475 ms** at the same ancestry: **≈ 0.001%**.

**And it takes no reading of its own** — asserted structurally in CI: the derivation runs over the already-materialized buffer with a counting `getBoundingClientRect` installed on the element, and the count is **zero**.

**D-85's recorded ground is confirmed, and that is the whole of what this arm does.** The marginal cost is arithmetic over a buffer `acquireLift` already produced, not a layout read — which is the ground the decision was accepted on, and F-65's shape does not point at it. **No timing result here could have removed `inheritedSpace`**, and none is offered as though it could: if the derivation had proved expensive, the finding would have been that the kernel derives it unconditionally, not that the derivation should stop.

## Telemetry — absolute activation latency

| ancestry                     | free drag        | sortable         |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------- | ---------------- |
| shallow                      | 0.073 – 0.087 ms | 0.094 – 0.100 ms |
| deep transformed (32 levels) | 0.453 – 0.491 ms | 0.425 – 0.475 ms |

Activation is **once per drag**. At the deepest ancestry tested it is ~3% of a 16 ms frame, and the two behaviors cost the same within the run-to-run spread — which is what a shared kernel activation path with two thin behavior effects should look like.

## What this closes, and what it does not

- **F-65 closes**, with both figures in its row, under the rule as written.
- **D-82's deferred SPI question does not open.** It opens on the absolute alone, over 0.5 ms; the measured absolute is under it by two orders of magnitude.
- **D-85 is untouched.** Arm B confirmed the ground it was accepted on and could not have done otherwise.
- **Nothing is implemented**, and no Phase 22 candidate is produced by this run.
- **§Check D-56 stays Phase R's** and is not recorded as satisfied here.

## What would reopen this

- A workload whose activation path makes window 1 a _first_ layout read rather than a second. The whole result rests on `getBoundingClientRect()` running on the statement before it; a kernel change that reordered those two would make window 1 a forced layout and this number meaningless.
- An ancestry far deeper than 32 levels, or one whose per-level cost is dominated by something other than a linear transform — although the mechanism above predicts window 1 stays flat while activation grows, which is what the shallow-versus-deep pair already shows.
- An engine whose offset-box accessors do not read from a flushed layout. The figures are Chromium's.
