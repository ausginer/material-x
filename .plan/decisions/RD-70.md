#### RD-70 — `mx-divider` is decorative by default, takes one enumerated `inset` and a vertical `orientation`, and a horizontal divider directly in `mx-card` reaches the card's edges

**Decided 2026-11-27 by the architect, on `material-x/divider-1`,** on the owner's answers to the [plan](../divider-1/divider-plan.md)'s two questions for #333, relayed by the coordinator: [Q1](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#q1--the-inset-attribute) (a), and [Q2](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#q2--a-divider-in-a-card) A2. `RD-69` is on `tproc/component-tables-1`, `RD-65` and `RD-66` are on `repo/mutations-1`, and `material-x/select-1` proposes an `RD-69` in its plan without minting it, so this is the next free number. **Unimplemented.** The plan's [_Work_](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#work) is ready for an implementer.

**The owner's words,** translated by the coordinator:

- **Q1 (a), `inset="start" | "middle"`:** _"Three booleans mean all three can technically be set at once, and then how do you tell which one applies 😅 That's why I gave up booleans like that in mx."_ And: _"And `variant` looks like extra noise, because it all comes down to inset anyway."_
- **The first reason is a rule for every component.** Asked whether to record it, he said: _"Yes, let's record it. It's an important parameter that I try to stick to."_ [`attribute-vs-state-styling.md` §Public Attributes](../../.agents/docs/attribute-vs-state-styling.md#public-attributes) states it, by `RD-71`, which lands with #337. This decision applies it.
- **Q2 A2:** a horizontal `mx-divider` that is a direct child of `mx-card` is `position: static`, so its line is positioned against the card's padding box.

**Properties.** The plan's [_The element_](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#the-element), [_Geometry_](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#geometry-as-required-properties), [_Accessibility_](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#accessibility) and [_Composition_](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#composition) are the full statement. In short:

1. **Surface.** `mx-divider` has two attributes, each with a property:
   - `inset`: `'start'` (16px at the start) or `'middle'` (16px at each side), and full-width when omitted;
   - `orientation`: `'horizontal'` or `'vertical'`, and horizontal when omitted.

   It has two CSS properties, `--md-divider-color` and `--md-divider-thickness`, and no slot, part, event or method. Each attribute resolves to a custom state. The 16px is a literal from M3's _Measurements_, because the store has no token for it. The divider has no margin of its own.

2. **The host is the box,** under the `.host` exception, which names `mx-divider`, and the shadow root holds no element. The line's color and thickness come from `md.comp.divider`'s two tokens. A page's universal reset changes neither the line nor its insets. In forced colors the line is `CanvasText`. Insets are logical, and on a vertical divider they apply from its block start.
3. **Decorative by default,** as M3 says: no role, no tab stop and no node in the accessibility tree, which is also what makes it valid directly in `mx-list`. A page that wants a separator announced sets `role="separator"` outside a list, and `orientation` sets the internals' `ariaOrientation`. A page's `aria-orientation` stands.
4. **In `mx-list`** a divider is a direct child between items, and the list doesn't change. **`mx-sortable-list` isn't supported,** because it would sort the divider with its items.
5. **In `mx-card`,** one rule in the card's stylesheet makes a horizontal `mx-divider` direct child `position: static`. The divider's line is an absolutely positioned box whose containing block is the divider's host, which is otherwise `position: relative`. So in the card it is positioned against the card's padding box, because the card is `position: relative` (`RD-53` clause 4). It reaches the card's edges with any `--md-card-padding`, and its inset is measured from the card's edge, as M3 measures it.
   - The rule needs no `!important`, because no universal reset sets `position`. A page's `position: relative` on the divider opts it out, and the line then spans the content box.
   - A vertical divider, a divider nested below a direct child, and all other content of the card are unchanged. So are the card's attributes, properties, padding, sizes, rows and stories.
   - **The two stylesheets have a contract:** the card relies on the divider's line being positioned against the divider's host. A comment in each stylesheet names the other, and a divider row and a card row keep it.
   - The rule selects `:state()` inside `::slotted()`, or the `orientation` attribute in an engine where that doesn't match, and the implementer's return says which.

**Why A2.** In the three engines, it is the only design read that meets M3's geometry for every `--md-card-padding` and leaves the rest of the card as it is ([readings](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#q2--a-divider-in-a-card)). A `::slotted` declaration loses to every page rule on the element, so one that a reset touches needs `!important`, and `position` is the one that no reset touches. A1's negative margin is invalid with two values of `--md-card-padding`. The owner's padding on the slotted content takes the inset from a text node and from a `display: contents` child, ignores a page's padding on a direct child, and moves `RD-53`'s contract.

**Not adopted:** Q1 (b), material-web's `inset`, `inset-start` and `inset-end`, and (c), `variant="inset" | "middle-inset"`. For Q2: P, padding on the slotted content; A1, a negative margin; and (b), nothing in the card. The plan has each with its reading.

**What `RD-53` says:** nothing in it changes. Its clause 8, _"No divider in this unit"_, said that a component is added when a page or a consumer asks for it, and #333 asks for this one.
