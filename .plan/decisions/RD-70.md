#### RD-70 — `mx-divider` is decorative by default, takes one enumerated `inset` and a vertical `orientation`, and a horizontal divider directly in `mx-card` reaches the card's edges

**Decided 2026-11-27 by the architect, on `material-x/divider-1`,** on the owner's answers to the [plan](../divider-1/divider-plan.md)'s two questions for #333, relayed by the coordinator: [Q1](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#q1--the-inset-attribute) (a), and [Q2](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#q2--a-divider-in-a-card) A2. `RD-69` is on `tproc/component-tables-1`, `RD-65` and `RD-66` are on `repo/mutations-1`, and `material-x/select-1` proposes an `RD-69` in its plan without minting it, so this is the next free number. **Unimplemented.** The plan's [_Work_](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#work) is ready for an implementer.

**Withdrawn by the second amendment below.** **Amended 2026-11-27, before merge, by the architect:** property 3's _"no role"_ becomes _"the internals' role is `none`"_. The implementer of this decision read the conflict in Chromium 158, relayed by the coordinator, and the architect read it again ([probe](../divider-1/probes/host-role.mjs), [reading](../divider-1/checks/host-role.txt)). **Unimplemented**, with the rest of the decision.

- **The conflict.** Without a role, Chromium gives the host an unignored `generic` node as soon as it holds an absolutely positioned line, or is a containing block for one: `position: relative`, `contain: layout` or a transform, with or without the `::before`. So does a static host with an absolutely positioned `::before`, which is the divider in `mx-card` (property 5). In an internals-`list` that puts a `generic` between the `listitem`s. A host with a static `::before`, or with its own background, has no node, but neither can reach the card's edges.
- **The ruling: `internals.role = 'none'`.** In every case above, Chromium then has no node, in an internals-`list` and in a plain container. A page's `role="separator"` gives a horizontal `separator`, and vertical when `orientation` sets the internals' `ariaOrientation`. A page's `aria-orientation` stands.
- **What doesn't change:** what the page sees and what assistive technology gets, which is property 3's "no node". The host has no `role` attribute, and the role is the component's default, under the page's.
- **A page's global ARIA attribute,** such as `aria-label`, overrides `none` by ARIA's rule for a presentational role, and the host is then a `generic`. The docs say that a decorative divider takes no label, and that one that should be announced takes `role="separator"`.
- **Firefox 159 and WebKit 27.2** accept `none` as the internals' role, and read it back. Neither engine's accessibility tree can be read with this repository's instruments: Playwright 1.65 reads it only over Chromium's CDP. So the rows read Chromium's tree, as the plan's rows already did, and the property holds in Firefox and WebKit by ARIA's rule for `none`, not by a reading.
- **Not adopted:** keep no role and drop the "no node" rows, which leaves a `generic` between a list's items. Another containing block for the line, which makes the same node in Chromium and doesn't remove the need for one.

**Amended again 2026-11-27, before merge, by the architect, on the owner's direction relayed by the coordinator: the divider is a simple box, and `mx-card` takes A1 with its padding split into an inline and a block property.** Properties 2, 3 and 5 and the reasons are replaced, and the amendment above is withdrawn. What they said is at the end. `RD-53` is amended with it, for the card's padding. **Unimplemented**, with the rest of the decision.

- **The owner's words,** translated by the coordinator. On the internals role: _"In my picture `mx-divider` is the simplest box, filled with CSS. Why does it have role none?"_ On A1's blocker, a `--md-card-padding` of two values: _"We just forbid it 😁 There will be a separate inline one and a separate block one."_
- **His premise holds** ([probe](../divider-1/probes/simple-box.mjs), [reading](../divider-1/checks/simple-box.txt), Chromium 158, Firefox 159 and WebKit 27.2). The node came only from the line that A2 needs: an absolutely positioned box, or a host that is its containing block. A host that is itself the line has no node in Chromium's tree, with no role: bare, in a container, in an internals-`list`, vertical in a flex row, and in a card. The card's own `generic`, which is there without a divider too, is `mx-card`'s, because it is `position: relative`, and isn't this decision's.
- **A1 now holds for every inline padding.** In the three engines, under a page's universal reset and without one, a horizontal direct child reaches the card's edges with the default padding, with `--md-card-padding-inline: 24px` and with `0`, and with a block padding that differs. `start` is 16px from the card's start edge and `middle` 16px from both, and they mirror under `dir="rtl"`. A vertical direct child and a divider in a child of the card are as they are outside a card. So `:state()` matches inside `::slotted()` in the three engines.
- **The one cost, as the owner weighed it:** the card's rule needs `!important` against a universal reset, so a page's inline margin on a horizontal direct child is ignored. Under A2, a page's inline margin didn't move the line either. A page's block margin applies.
- **The amendment above is withdrawn:** the internals have no role. Until this amendment, property 3 said _"the internals' role is `none`"_.

**The owner's words,** translated by the coordinator:

- **Q1 (a), `inset="start" | "middle"`:** _"Three booleans mean all three can technically be set at once, and then how do you tell which one applies 😅 That's why I gave up booleans like that in mx."_ And: _"And `variant` looks like extra noise, because it all comes down to inset anyway."_
- **The first reason is a rule for every component.** Asked whether to record it, he said: _"Yes, let's record it. It's an important parameter that I try to stick to."_ [`attribute-vs-state-styling.md` §Public Attributes](../../.agents/docs/attribute-vs-state-styling.md#public-attributes) states it, by `RD-71`, which lands with #337. This decision applies it.
- **Q2 A2, then A1:** a horizontal `mx-divider` that is a direct child of `mx-card` was `position: static`, so its line was positioned against the card's padding box. The owner then chose A1 with the card's padding split, in the second amendment above.

**Properties.** The plan's [_The element_](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#the-element), [_Geometry_](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#geometry-as-required-properties), [_Accessibility_](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#accessibility) and [_Composition_](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#composition) are the full statement. In short:

1. **Surface.** `mx-divider` has two attributes, each with a property:
   - `inset`: `'start'` (16px at the start) or `'middle'` (16px at each side), and full-width when omitted;
   - `orientation`: `'horizontal'` or `'vertical'`, and horizontal when omitted.

   It has two CSS properties, `--md-divider-color` and `--md-divider-thickness`, and no slot, part, event or method. Each attribute resolves to a custom state. The 16px is a literal from M3's _Measurements_, because the store has no token for it. The divider has no margin of its own.

2. **The host is the box, and it is the line,** under the `.host` exception, which names `mx-divider`. The shadow root holds no element, and nothing is a pseudo-element, positioned or a containing block.
   - **The line is the host's own background,** clipped to its content box. Its color and thickness come from `md.comp.divider`'s two tokens: a horizontal host's block size is the thickness, and a vertical host's inline size.
   - **The insets are the host's own padding,** logical, and on a vertical divider along the line from its block start. The host's padding, its `background-clip` and its `box-sizing: content-box` are `!important`, so a page's universal reset and a page's `padding` on the divider move neither the line nor its insets. A page's `margin` on the divider applies, except as property 5 says.
   - **In forced colors** the line is `CanvasText`. Without that rule, forced colors paint the host's background `Canvas`, and the line disappears ([reading](../divider-1/checks/simple-box.txt), its last section).
3. **Decorative by default,** as M3 says: no role, no tab stop and no node in the accessibility tree, which is also what makes it valid directly in `mx-list`. A page that wants a separator announced sets `role="separator"` outside a list, and `orientation` sets the internals' `ariaOrientation`. A page's `aria-orientation` stands.
4. **In `mx-list`** a divider is a direct child between items, and the list doesn't change. **`mx-sortable-list` isn't supported,** because it would sort the divider with its items.
5. **In `mx-card`,** one rule in the card's stylesheet gives a horizontal `mx-divider` direct child a negative inline margin of the card's inline padding, `!important`: `::slotted(mx-divider:not(:state(vertical))) { margin-inline: calc(-1 * var(--md-card-padding-inline, 16px)) !important; }`. Its box then spans the card's padding box, and its inset is measured from the card's edge, as M3 measures it.
   - **It reads one length.** `--md-card-padding-inline` and `--md-card-padding-block` replace `--md-card-padding` (`RD-53`'s amendment of 2026-11-27), so the `calc()` is valid for every value the card supports.
   - A page's inline margin on a horizontal direct child is ignored, and its block margin applies. A vertical divider, a divider nested below a direct child, and all other content of the card are unchanged.
   - **The two stylesheets have a contract:** the card's rule pulls out the divider's box, and the divider draws its line and its insets inside that box. A comment in each stylesheet names the other, and a divider row and a card row keep it.

**Why the simple box and A1.** It meets M3's geometry in a card and outside one, in the three engines, with no node and no role, and no element or pseudo-element in the shadow tree. The line needs no containing block, so nothing in the card depends on how the divider paints. A1 was blocked only by a padding of two values, which the split removes. A1's cost, a page's inline margin ignored on a direct child, is A2's too.

**Not adopted:** Q1 (b), material-web's `inset`, `inset-start` and `inset-end`, and (c), `variant="inset" | "middle-inset"`. For Q2: P, padding on the slotted content; A2, a static divider whose absolutely positioned line the card holds, which gives the host a node in Chromium unless its internals' role is `none`; and (b), nothing in the card. A static `::before` that draws the line has no node either ([reading](../divider-1/checks/host-role.txt)), but a page's `*::before` reset reaches it, so each of its declarations needs `!important`, and the host's own box is the simpler. The plan has each with its reading.

**What `RD-53` says:** its clause 1 and its padding change with this decision's second amendment: `--md-card-padding` becomes `--md-card-padding-inline` and `--md-card-padding-block`. Its clause 8, _"No divider in this unit"_, said that a component is added when a page or a consumer asks for it, and #333 asks for this one. Until the second amendment, this said that nothing in `RD-53` changes.

- Property 5:

  > **In `mx-card`,** one rule in the card's stylesheet makes a horizontal `mx-divider` direct child `position: static`. The divider's line is an absolutely positioned box whose containing block is the divider's host, which is otherwise `position: relative`. So in the card it is positioned against the card's padding box, because the card is `position: relative` (`RD-53` clause 4). It reaches the card's edges with any `--md-card-padding`, and its inset is measured from the card's edge, as M3 measures it.
  >

  > - A vertical divider, a divider nested below a direct child, and all other content of the card are unchanged. So are the card's attributes, properties, padding, sizes, rows and stories.
  > - **The two stylesheets have a contract:** the card relies on the divider's line being positioned against the divider's host. A comment in each stylesheet names the other, and a divider row and a card row keep it.
  > - The rule selects `:state()` inside `::slotted()`, or the `orientation` attribute in an engine where that doesn't match, and the implementer's return says which.

- **Why A2.** In the three engines, it is the only design read that meets M3's geometry for every `--md-card-padding` and leaves the rest of the card as it is ([readings](../divider-1/divider-plan.md#q2--a-divider-in-a-card)). A `::slotted` declaration loses to every page rule on the element, so one that a reset touches needs `!important`, and `position` is the one that no reset touches. A1's negative margin is invalid with two values of `--md-card-padding`. The owner's padding on the slotted content takes the inset from a text node and from a `display: contents` child, ignores a page's padding on a direct child, and moves `RD-53`'s contract.
- Not adopted, for Q2: _"P, padding on the slotted content; A1, a negative margin; and (b), nothing in the card."_
